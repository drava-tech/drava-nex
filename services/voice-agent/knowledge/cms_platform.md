# CMS Platform

## What the CMS is

<!-- say: what the CMS is -->

The CMS is the control system for this experience centre, built by White Tusker. From one web page, an operator controls what plays on every screen, runs interactive experiences, and operates the room itself: lights, air conditioning, sound and the door. It is built for showrooms and experience centres, not for public websites.

## Screens

<!-- say: screens -->

Every physical screen in the room is registered in the CMS and can be controlled on its own. An operator can show an experience or a media item on a screen, play a queue of videos, pause, skip, blank the screen to black, or reset it to the start. When something is assigned, the screen changes straight away. If a screen loses power or its network, it comes back showing what it was meant to show.

## Experiences

<!-- say: experiences -->

An experience is a piece of interactive content built from a template, such as a welcome screen, a portfolio, a product showcase, a gallery, a flight simulation, a live stream or object recognition. Each experience keeps its own content, which operators edit with a live preview. The same experience can be shown on any screen.

## Media and presentations

<!-- say: media -->

The media library holds videos, images and documents such as presentations. Media can be uploaded, added from a web link, or arrive as an email attachment. An operator picks an item and pushes it to one or more screens, or queues several items to play one after another.

## Controlling the room

<!-- say: room controls -->

The CMS also runs the room's equipment. Operators can switch lights on and off, set brightness and colour, change the air conditioning, adjust the TV volume with its remote, and lock or unlock the door. Travis uses the same controls, so what you ask Travis to do is what the CMS does.

## Room modes

<!-- say: room modes -->

The room has ready-made lighting modes for the stages of a visit: pre-entry, entry, meeting and presentation. Choosing a mode sets the lights for that moment in one step, and the operator can adjust the light levels afterwards.

## Products and portfolio

<!-- say: products and portfolio -->

The CMS keeps product details with their features, specifications and media, which feed the product showcase. It also holds the White Tusker portfolio, organised by verticals, companies and projects with images and videos, which can be shown on the main screen.

## Whiteboards

<!-- say: whiteboards -->

Operators can create multi-page whiteboards and draw on them live. The strokes appear on the chosen screens as they are drawn, so a screen can be used like a shared board during a meeting.

## Who can use it

<!-- say: who can use it -->

Operators sign in from a web browser. The CMS has users and roles, so administrators can create and edit content while others only operate the room. The screens themselves need no sign-in; they simply show what has been assigned to them.

## What you can say to Travis

<!-- say: what you can say to Travis -->

Say Hey Travis, then a request, such as turn off the cove light, set the AC to 22, lock the door, lower the volume, or stop the main screen. Travis can also show an experience or media item on a screen. Travis only uses the names of devices and screens that really exist in this room.
